# Lead Onboarding Optimization Pattern

## When To Use

Use this pattern when evidence shows slow response, unclear ownership, repeated qualification work, missing follow-up, or a gap between inquiry and booked next step.

Do not assume the business needs a specific CRM or automated customer messaging. Establish the current workflow and leakage first.

## Map The Current Flow

For each step, record:

- Trigger and source
- Owner
- System or document used
- Waiting and work time
- Decision rule
- Handoff and exception
- Conversion, error, and abandonment rate
- Customer and compliance impact

## Minimum Future Capability

A credible solution may need:

- One shared lead record and accountable owner
- Response and follow-up status
- Qualification fields and documented rules
- Estimate, appointment, or next-step scheduling
- Source attribution and outcome reporting
- Safe exception handling and human override

Research current vendor or build options only after these requirements are clear. Include setup cost, recurring cost, implementation time, migration effort, security, data ownership, and fit for the actual team.

## Proof

1. Backtest historical inquiries to estimate missed follow-up and recoverable value.
2. Simulate capacity and ownership changes when the historical data is incomplete.
3. Run the proposed workflow in shadow mode beside the current process.
4. Pilot on a small approved subset before automating external messages or production writes.

## KPIs

- Time to first meaningful response
- Inquiry-to-next-step rate
- Qualified lead rate
- Follow-up completion
- No-show and cancellation rate
- Employee time per lead
- Close rate and gross profit per lead
- Duplicate, incorrect, or missed actions
- Complaints, opt-outs, and compliance exceptions

Approval is required before customer communication, CRM writes, spend, or production rollout.
